## Why

`strip-thinking-preamble@v1` advanced past the first line even when no duplicated preamble
was present. Demonstrated by the non-Claude oracle and reproduced immediately:

```
'Finding A\nFinding B'                     ->  'Finding B'
'Opening paragraph.\n\nSecond.\n\nThird.'  ->  'Second.\n\nThird.'
```

This particular message DID carry a preamble, so v1 stripped it and then took one line too
many — the same off-by-one, on the paragraph that followed.

## Why nothing caught it

Both checks examined the text that survived and neither asked whether anything had been
removed:

- My digest was computed over the extractor's OUTPUT, so it certified the truncated text as
  faithfully as it would have certified the whole.
- The chat lane read the file against its own memory of what it wrote and reported it
  unaltered, start to finish. Its reconstruction was loose enough — by its own earlier
  admission it has no byte-perfect copy — that a missing opening paragraph did not register.

Two independent verifications, both of the wrong property. Persisting the output of a lossy
extractor cannot recover what the extractor never emitted, and the rendered source is gone
within about two minutes.
